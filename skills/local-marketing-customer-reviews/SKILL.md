# Reviews

## Purpose and inputs

Run a post-project review workflow with neutral requests, follow-up tracking, response drafts, and customer issue escalation. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Completed-project list, approved review destinations, contact permissions, recent reviews, request history, and the customer-service owner. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Match completed projects to customers and prior requests. Deduplicate contacts and exclude opt-outs; use consistent eligibility rather than selecting only happy customers.

2. Draft a short request referencing the completed work and inviting an honest review. Do not offer a reward for a positive review or tell the customer what rating to leave.

3. Prepare one polite reminder for eligible nonresponders after the agreed interval. Stop reminders after a review, refusal, opt-out, or unresolved service issue.

4. Capture review source, date, rating, text, and project association. Draft tailored responses; immediately flag complaints with the facts and a proposed private follow-up. Obtain permission where needed before reusing a testimonial.

## Deliverable

Return a request tracker with customer ID, project, channel, initial request, reminder due, review link, opt-out, and issue owner; include ready-to-review messages and response drafts.

## Quality checks

Do not fabricate testimonials or expose customer records in public outputs. Treat unresolved complaints as service work, not a reason to pressure someone into changing a rating.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
