# Content research

## Purpose and inputs

Turn real homeowner questions into a prioritized content backlog and practical on-camera briefs. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Service specialties, geography, existing content, customer/sales questions, and access to public search, forum, or video sources. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Collect questions from actual calls or supplied notes and public search, Reddit, and YouTube material. Save sources and dates; remove identifying customer details.

2. Cluster duplicate questions by homeowner problem and buying stage. Separate awareness questions, comparison concerns, cost/process questions, and care needs.

3. Prioritize topics by relevance, repeated evidence, business expertise, and a gap in existing content. Do not fabricate search volumes or claim a question is common from one example.

4. Prepare a filming brief with the exact question, a useful opening, answer outline, proof/example needed, suggested visuals, and a modest CTA. Flag technical answers for field-team review.

## Deliverable

Return a sourced question bank, ranked editorial backlog, and ready-to-film briefs with target audience, evidence, owner, and proposed channel.

## Quality checks

Draft original answers rather than copying source passages. Mark uncertainty and avoid construction, safety, or pricing claims the business cannot substantiate.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
