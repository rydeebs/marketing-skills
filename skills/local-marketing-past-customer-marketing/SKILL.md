# Past-customer marketing

## Purpose and inputs

Find appropriate maintenance, repeat-service, and referral opportunities in past-customer records and prepare personal check-ins. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Completed-project history, product/care records, contact permissions, prior issues, last outreach dates, and verified service offerings. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Build a customer/project history with completion date, installed materials, relevant care guidance, and prior visits. Keep unsupported dates or product details explicitly unknown.

2. Estimate an outreach window from verified product guidance and actual project conditions. Distinguish a suggested inspection from a claim that work is due.

3. Prioritize relevant customers by need and recency of contact; exclude opt-outs and route open complaints to customer service. Separate maintenance offers, simple check-ins, and referral requests.

4. Draft a personal message referencing only known project facts. Log the proposed next action and stop cadence when the customer responds, declines, or opts out.

## Deliverable

Deliver a customer opportunity table with project ID, reason, evidence, contact eligibility, message draft, last touch, next date, and owner.

## Quality checks

Do not invent maintenance intervals or diagnose structural conditions from marketing data. A campaign is complete only when outcomes are recorded as well as contacts attempted.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
