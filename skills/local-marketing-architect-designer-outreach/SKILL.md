# Architect / Designer outreach

## Purpose and inputs

Build relationships with relevant architects and designers through sourced research and tailored portfolio introductions. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Service geography, craft specialties, approved project portfolio, target project types, existing relationships, and CRM records. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Research local architecture, landscape architecture, and design practices. Capture a public project or specialization that establishes a genuine fit.

2. Identify an appropriate business contact and record source, role, contact confidence, and existing relationship history. Avoid redundant contacts across the same firm.

3. Select one portfolio example that addresses the firm’s work. Draft a short introduction explaining execution capability, coordination approach, and a modest next step.

4. Prepare thoughtful social engagement only where the business has something useful to add. Track replies, meetings, specification opportunities, and next actions instead of rewarding empty activity.

## Deliverable

Deliver a prioritized firm list, contact evidence, portfolio match, outreach drafts, and relationship tracker.

## Quality checks

Do not pretend to know a designer or compliment work you have not inspected. Respect image rights and avoid making technical or schedule commitments without operational confirmation.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
