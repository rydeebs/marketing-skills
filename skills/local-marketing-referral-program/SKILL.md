# Referral program

## Purpose and inputs

Maintain referral partner relationships, lead handoffs, and follow-up records for a local service business. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Existing partners, ideal lead criteria, service area, approved portfolio, referral terms if any, and CRM lead records. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Create or refresh partner records with organization, relationship owner, specialty, fit, last touch, and referrals received. Preserve the original referral source on each opportunity.

2. Define a concise ideal-referral brief: service needed, geography, scope, timing, and contact route. State exclusions so partners can recognize poor-fit leads.

3. Prepare relevant project updates and individualized check-ins based on the relationship. Do not invent a referral incentive or promise compensation absent approved terms.

4. Track each referred lead through acknowledgment, qualification, appointment, and outcome. Prepare partner updates that respect the customer’s confidentiality and record the next follow-up.

## Deliverable

Deliver a partner register, ideal-referral brief, communication drafts, and a referral funnel with source, stage, owner, and next action.

## Quality checks

Deduplicate partner and customer records. Separate referred opportunities from won jobs and collected revenue; do not infer a successful referral from a message being sent.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
