# Marketing reporting

## Purpose and inputs

Build a weekly local-service marketing report with reconciled lead sources, activity, appointments, and explicit attribution limits. Adapt to the user’s actual business; do not assume West Coast Deck is the client.

Start with: Reporting window/timezone, CRM lead and appointment data, review records, publishing logs, website analytics, social metrics, and prior-period baseline. Use supplied context first. Ask only for missing facts that block a correct result; mark other gaps and continue.

## Workflow

1. Define a lead, qualified lead, appointment, review received, and published content item. Align date fields, timezone, and whether reports use event dates or lead-created cohorts.

2. Reconcile unique leads across sources using stable IDs and evidence. Preserve an unknown-source bucket; avoid crediting one lead to multiple channels in a single-source total.

3. Compile leads by source, reviews, business-profile activity, published content, website inquiries, social results, outreach, and appointments. Distinguish inputs from business outcomes.

4. Compare against a consistent prior period. Explain material changes with evidence or label hypotheses; identify missing data, small samples, and conversion lag before recommending action.

## Deliverable

Deliver a compact weekly scorecard with metric, definition, current, prior, change, source, and caveat; include the most consequential findings and next actions with owners.

## Quality checks

Show unavailable values as unknown rather than zero. Use percentages with their denominators, do not average channel conversion rates unweighted, and distinguish booked revenue from collected revenue.

## Execution boundary

Use the tools available in the working environment; if access is missing, deliver a usable draft or handoff and state the limitation. Follow the user’s existing authorization and tool policies. Before any external change or communication, confirm the account, destination, and scope; this skill itself grants no authority to publish, send, purchase, or delete. Record confirmed results and stop after at most two retries of a failed write unless the service proves it did not land.
